My Opinion: Two-Layer Architecture
You're absolutely right, and the data proves it. Here's what I think the architecture should be:

Layer 1: Thesis Universe (You curate, ~50–80 stocks)
This is your unfair advantage. No algorithm can replicate this. Characteristics of stocks in this universe:

Identifiable growth thesis — defense allocation, EMS sector boom, space economy, capex cycle, PSU turnaround, specialty chemicals, EV supply chain
Management execution — order book visibility, revenue growth trajectory, margin stability
Sector tailwinds — government policy, import substitution, global re-shoring
Volatility — mid/small cap (naturally 40–60% annual vol, which the data shows is REQUIRED for big moves)
You update this quarterly. Some stocks rotate in (new thesis emerging), some rotate out (thesis played out or broken).

Layer 2: Apollo Engine (Automated, runs on your universe)
The bucket classifier's job changes from "should I include this stock?" to:

Structural Decline → Skip (even if in your universe, the thesis may be temporarily broken)
Early Recovery → Standard position, wider SL (the high-opportunity zone)
Recovery Strengthening → Full position, tighter SL (confirmed recovery)
Trending Up → Pullback entries only (wait for RSI < 40 within the trend)
The scoring engine doesn't change. It still does its 21-signal evaluation. But the universe filter dramatically improves your hit rate because you're fishing in a pond where you know the fish exist.

Why this is better than pure technical bucketing of all NSE stocks:

At the low point, Apollo Micro and a dying small-cap look IDENTICAL technically — both have RSI 34, both 30%+ below 200 DMA, both with falling MAs. The only difference is the thesis. The bucket classifier can't tell them apart. But YOU can.

So instead of trying to make the algorithm smarter about stock selection (which is fundamentally limited), you restrict the universe to stocks you've already vetted, and let Apollo handle the timing — which is what it's designed for.

Practically: The bucket classifier still gets built (we agreed on this). But it becomes a risk management and position sizing tool, not a stock selection tool. You can have a "Universe Only" toggle in the UI that restricts backtests to your curated list.

My Opinion: Yes, You're Absolutely Right — IPOs Get Penalised Unfairly
The problem is severe. 43% of the total possible score (48 out of 113 points) depends on an RSI trough being detected. For a recently listed IPO with, say, 40–60 trading days:

A trough may never form — the stock hasn't traded long enough to dip RSI(21) below 40 while also being a centered rolling minimum within an 11-bar window. The detect_troughs() function uses a centered rolling window (center=True), which needs 5 bars on both sides — impossible in the first ~31 bars.
Even if one does form, it's likely an artefact of listing-day volatility, not a genuine "recovery from oversold" — the whole narrative of "trough → recovery" doesn't apply to a stock that just IPO'd.
The scoring penalty is huge and silent — the backtest doesn't flag "insufficient history for trough". It just scores 0 on 7 signals and the IPO appears as a low-score stock, not as a "can't evaluate properly" stock.
What I'd Recommend: Three-Pronged Approach
1. IPO Detection Gate (Layer 0 — before even Layer 1 pre-filter)
Detect IPOs by listing date (from the data itself or from a config parameter like min_trading_days). If a stock has fewer than ~60 trading days (roughly 3 months), flag it as IPO / New Listing. This is clean and explicit.

2. Alternative Scoring Path for IPOs (not just "skip")
Instead of the trough-recovery narrative, evaluate IPOs on what is observable:

Trend Strength: 20-DMA slope, price vs 20-DMA (no 200-DMA needed)
Volume Confirmation: Volume trend, OBV
RSI Level (not trough-based): Current RSI(21) zone — is it in a healthy 50–70 range?
Volatility & Range: ATR, narrowing/widening bands
Price Structure: Higher highs/higher lows since listing
This would be a dedicated "IPO scoring profile" — maybe 10–12 signals instead of 21, focused on momentum and trend establishment rather than recovery.

3. Trough-Dependent Signals Get IPO Substitutes
For the 7 trough-dependent signals, create IPO-equivalent alternatives:

Original Signal
Points
IPO Substitute
A1: Trough Detected (D)	10	"Listing Consolidation" — price settled into a range after listing volatility
A2: Trough Detected (4H)	8	"4H RSI Stability" — RSI(21) held above 45 for N bars
A3: Recovery Momentum (D)	8	"Post-Listing Uptrend" — consistent higher highs
A4: Recovery Momentum (4H)	6	"4H Momentum Building" — RSI trending up

This preserves the scoring weight distribution (~43% for this cluster) so IPO scores are comparable to mature stock scores.

Why Not Just Skip IPOs?
Skipping is the easy answer, but IPOs are often where the biggest moves happen. A dedicated evaluation path gives you actionable signals on high-momentum new listings rather than blindly ignoring them.

Phase 3.5: Making the Engine Auto-Start (systemd)
Goal: The engine should start automatically when the VPS boots up and restart automatically if it crashes. You don't want to manually start it every time.

How it works: Linux has a built-in service manager called systemd. Think of it as Windows Task Scheduler on steroids. You create a small configuration file that says "run this Python script, keep it alive, restart it if it dies." Then the OS handles everything.

What you need to do:

Create a file called apollo-live.service by typing:

bash

This opens a text editor. Paste this in (I'll give you the exact content when we get here):

ini

[Unit]
Description=APOLLO Live Trading Engine
After=network.target

[Service]
Type=simple
User=root
WorkingDirectory=/root/APOLLO-BACKTEST
ExecStart=/usr/bin/python3 live_engine/live_app.py
Restart=always
RestartSec=10

[Install]
WantedBy=multi-user.target
What this says in plain English:

"After the network is connected, start this service"
"Run python3 live_engine/live_app.py from the project directory"
"If it crashes, wait 10 seconds and restart it automatically"
Then activate it:

# Enable it to start on boot
